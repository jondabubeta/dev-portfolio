# Why I Chose Graduate School

**Published:** June 22, 2026

Deciding to apply for a master's degree was not about stepping away from software engineering. It was about following the direction the field is moving and preparing myself to contribute to it with greater depth.

Over the past several years, artificial intelligence and machine learning have moved from specialized research areas into everyday software products. Companies are investing in intelligent systems, automation, model-driven features, and the infrastructure required to support them. That shift is changing what software engineers build, how we build it, and which skills will matter most in the years ahead.

I wanted to understand that change instead of only watching it happen.

## Why Now

My experience in software engineering, quality engineering, testing, and data analysis has taught me how to build systems and ask whether they work as intended. AI and ML introduce a deeper set of questions: how a system learns, how its behavior is measured, where its data creates limitations, and how it can be evaluated responsibly.

Graduate school felt like the right next step because I wanted a structured environment in which to study those questions. Independent learning has helped me explore new tools and ideas, but a rigorous curriculum offers something different: stronger theory, research practice, expert guidance, and the expectation that I defend my reasoning.

## The Curriculum I Want to Build

My goal is to connect computer science fundamentals with the practical realities of modern AI systems. The areas I want my graduate work to cover include:

- Machine-learning foundations, algorithms, and model evaluation
- Artificial intelligence and deep-learning techniques
- Data engineering, preparation, and statistical analysis
- Responsible AI, fairness testing, reliability, and explainability
- Research methods and the ability to evaluate technical claims
- Applied projects that integrate models into dependable software systems

This curriculum is not only about learning how to train a model. I want to understand the complete system around it: the data, infrastructure, testing strategy, user impact, and engineering decisions that determine whether it is genuinely useful.

## Building on Software Engineering

I do not see AI as replacing the need for software engineering. If anything, intelligent systems make strong engineering practices more important. Models still need reliable applications, maintainable code, thoughtful interfaces, observability, security, and testing.

My aim is to bring those disciplines together. I want to build on the engineering experience I already have while developing the mathematical, analytical, and research skills needed to work confidently with AI and ML.

## Following the Direction of the Industry

No one can predict exactly where the industry will go, but the investment is clear. AI is becoming part of how software is designed, developed, tested, and used. I decided that I wanted to participate in shaping that future, not simply adapt to it after the fact.

Applying for a master's degree was my commitment to that decision. It gave me a curriculum, a direction, and a reason to pursue the questions that have increasingly captured my attention. The degree is an important milestone, but the real goal is the knowledge behind it and what I will be able to build with it.
